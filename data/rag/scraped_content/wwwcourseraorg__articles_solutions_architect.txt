What is a solutions architect?
A solutions architect develops the technical vision for a solution to a business problem. Guided by business requirements, they design, describe, and manage software architecture's technological solutions. These tech professionals build the bridge between business and technical expertise to outline each of the phases and requirements required to reach business goals.
What does a solutions architect do?
A good solutions architect analyzes the existing environment and determines what technologies are available and what software product must be developed to provide the best solution for the problem that needs to be solved. From there, the solutions architect creates an overall strategic technical vision, similar to an architect designing a blueprint for a building. Based on that vision, the solutions architect also develops a budget for producing the software product.
After the
stakeholders
have agreed on the project, the solutions architect is responsible for monitoring the process and keeping stakeholders updated and informed on the progress. Most of the time, the project involves technical and non-technical stakeholders, and the solutions architect must ensure that each party's needs are considered and factored into the project's scope.

Solutions architect vs. project manager
Although a solutions architect is not a project manager, this position requires excellent
project management skills
to ensure that the project stays on time, on budget, and effectively solves the problem it was developed to solve. It also requires clear
communication skills
and the ability to translate technical details of the project into simple language that will be easy for non-technical stakeholders to understand. Successful solutions architects are usually curious, rational, analytical, and methodical, all of which are essential traits for this job.
The solutions architect role also requires being able to project into the future and consider upcoming uses. A solutions architect must view the long-term use of this product and implement scalability and adaptability into the solution for the future. If your career path is heading towards a solutions architect role, you might consider boosting your resume with some
technical project management
experience.

If you are creative, analytical, outgoing, pragmatic, and can explain problems and solutions on both a technical and non-technical level, you could have a future as a solutions architect.
